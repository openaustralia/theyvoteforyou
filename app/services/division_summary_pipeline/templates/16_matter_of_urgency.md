**Jargon Explainer:** *The Senate has two ways of setting aside time for a pressing issue. A matter of public importance is a discussion with no question before the chair, so no vote is taken on it. A matter of urgency is a motion, in the form "That in the opinion of the Senate the following is a matter of urgency", and it is voted on, which is why this one produced a division. It needs four senators besides the proposer to support it before it can proceed. Technically the vote decides only whether the subject is urgent, but Odgers' Australian Senate Practice notes that it is often regarded as a vote on the substance of the matter itself, because the motion can be worded so that either side of the vote carries a meaning.*
