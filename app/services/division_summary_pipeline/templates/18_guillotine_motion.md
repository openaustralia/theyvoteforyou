**Jargon Explainer:** *A limitation of debate, often called a 'guillotine', caps the time available so the remaining stages are put to a vote whether or not debate has finished. In the Senate, when the time expires the chair puts the remaining questions immediately, including questions on amendments circulated at least two hours beforehand, even if nobody has moved them; anything circulated later can only be considered by leave. In the House of Representatives a Minister declares a bill urgent, and the question "That the bill be considered urgent" is put immediately, without amendment or debate. If it is agreed to, a single second reading debate runs from 7.30 pm until 10 pm that sitting, and at the next sitting the questions on any second reading amendment and on the second reading are put without further debate; circulated amendments are then treated as moved and put in groups (the government's, then the opposition's, then each crossbench member's), and the remaining stages follow without delay. Once a time limit is in place the closure ("That the question be now put") cannot be moved for the proceedings it covers, because the timetable has already replaced it.*

*This vote is about how much time the chamber spends on the business, not about the business itself.*
